A furnishing enquiry uncovered a better way to use the water view in the home.

The Invisible Layer Series No.9

Before Design Begins

Early design involvement allows the property, the client’s priorities and the decisions ahead to influence the project before major decisions are fixed.

During one Design Consultation, the main bedroom stood out as we moved through the property because it had the best water view in the home, yet the layout made very little use of it.

The original orientation missed the strongest outlook in the room.

In its existing orientation, the view was immediately visible on entry, but when lying in bed, you were left staring at a wall.

Re-positioning the doorway changed how the room related to the view.

We redesigned the bedroom, moved the door and reworked the layout so the bed faced the view. The new orientation changed how the room was experienced every day.

The outlook becomes part of the space.

Our Design Consultation allows us to understand the client’s needs and assess the opportunities and weaknesses within the property and existing layout before defining the design brief. The brief is grounded in what we have seen and discussed, giving the project a meaningful starting point.

Across two hours, we review the property, objectives, priorities and available information, including plans, inspiration imagery, existing conditions and decisions already under consideration. From there, we identify what needs closer review, where the opportunities and constraints sit and which service pathway makes sense before larger commitments are made. The consultation provides professional guidance around direction, scope and next steps before the project moves into design work.

After the consultation, we prepare a tailored proposal outlining the recommended service pathway, proposed scope of works, design fee estimate and next-step recommendations.

The Invisible Layer

A meaningful design brief gives the project clear parameters. It directs design effort and investment towards the decisions with the greatest impact, while giving the builder and wider project team a shared set of goals to reference.

It also prevents the project from being shaped by assumptions made before the property was understood. The advice is specific to the client, the property and the issues identified, reducing the risk of rushed input leading to decisions that must be corrected later.
